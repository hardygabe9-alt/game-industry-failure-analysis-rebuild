# Research methodology

## Research question
How do selected video games released between 2008 and 2026 compare across commercial performance, critic reception, player reception, development/production context, and operational lifecycle?

## Unit of analysis
A single game or clearly identified expansion. Keep expansions separate from base games when evidence differs. A franchise-level claim must not be silently applied to every entry.

## Outcome categories
- Commercial: sales, revenue, profitability, or performance against documented expectations.
- Operational: shutdown, cancellation, or major service interruption.
- Critical reception: professional critic scores.
- Player reception: player ratings/reviews and broader sentiment, with sampling limitations.
- Development: documented production or organizational problems.
- Lifecycle: ongoing support, content cadence, active development, and sunset status.

## Evidence hierarchy
1. Regulatory filing or direct official publisher/developer statement.
2. Official sales chart or named market-research chart with geography, period, and metric.
3. Credible reporting that identifies its source and method.
4. Analyst estimate, clearly labelled as an estimate.
5. User-generated discussion as qualitative evidence only, not a standalone sales or causal claim.

## Comparability rules
- Do not compare global lifetime units with annual U.S. dollar-sales ranks as if they were the same metric.
- Preserve the source's terminology: sold-in, sold-through, copies purchased, revenue, active users, and subscriptions are not interchangeable.
- Record date, geography, platform, edition, and time window where available.
- Distinguish launch results from lifetime outcomes.
- Do not infer profitability from sales alone without costs, revenue share, marketing, and monetization data.
- Do not infer a commercial failure from low review scores, online criticism, or shutdown alone.
- For “expected vs actual,” record a dated pre-release expectation and a comparable actual metric. If no expectation is documented, mark the gap as unavailable rather than inventing a target.
- Treat 2026 data as provisional because the year is incomplete.

## Bias and alternative explanations
Consider price, platform reach, genre, marketing, release timing, competition, franchise strength, live-service design, technical quality, accessibility, monetization, and subscription availability. Treat claims about representation/DEIA and player behavior as hypotheses to test with evidence, not assumptions.
